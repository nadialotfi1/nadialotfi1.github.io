Oscillometric Blood Pressure Monitoring System

Biomedical Instrumentation · 2026

Designed and analyzed an instrumentation system for non-invasive blood-pressure measurement using pressure sensing, analog amplification and filtering, and Python-based physiological signal processing.

Biomedical Instrumentation Python Signal Processing Analog Circuits
System Overview

From cuff pressure to physiological measurements

The system converts pressure changes in an inflatable cuff into an electrical signal that can be amplified, filtered, recorded, and analyzed.

One channel preserves the raw cuff-pressure waveform, while a second filtered channel isolates the small oscillations produced by arterial pulsation.

Instrumentation

Amplifying and isolating the oscillometric signal

The pressure sensor output was amplified using an INA121 instrumentation amplifier. The amplified signal was split into a raw-pressure channel and a filtered channel.

High-pass and low-pass filtering removed the slow cuff pressure trend and higher-frequency noise, making the pulse-related oscillations easier to analyze.

Signal Acquisition

Separating cuff pressure from arterial oscillations

The raw signal captures inflation and gradual deflation of the cuff, while the filtered signal reveals the smaller pressure oscillations produced by blood flow.

As cuff pressure falls, the oscillation envelope grows, reaches a maximum, and then decreases — the pattern used for oscillometric blood-pressure estimation.

Signal Processing

Extracting the oscillation envelope

Python was used to trim the useful portion of the signal, detect oscillatory peaks and troughs, and calculate peak-to-peak amplitudes.

The largest oscillation amplitude was used to identify mean arterial pressure, while percentage thresholds on either side of the maximum were used to estimate systolic and diastolic pressure.

Estimated Blood Pressure

Extracting MAP, systolic, and diastolic pressure

Systolic 135.7 mmHg
Mean Arterial Pressure 89.3 mmHg
Diastolic 70.4 mmHg

What I learned

Physiological signal processing is highly sensitive to noise and peak-detection choices. The heart-rate estimates from both the frequency-domain analysis and automated peak detection were higher than physiologically expected, highlighting the need for more robust filtering and peak-selection methods.

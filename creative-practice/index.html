Creative Practice

Making, testing, and learning through materials.

My creative practice and engineering work are shaped by many of the same habits: observation, prototyping, testing, and refinement.

I’ve learned a lot from ideas that looked good on paper but changed once they encountered real materials, real constraints, or real people. Those moments have made me a better builder — more willing to question assumptions, understand materials deeply, and treat iteration as an essential part of the process.

Whether I’m working in ceramics, sketching an idea, or building an engineering prototype, I’m most interested in thoughtful, human-centered design that feels tangible, useful, and connected to the people it is made for.

Reflection

Making keeps ideas honest.

Working by hand reminds me that good design is rarely finished in the first iteration. Materials push back, prototypes fail, and feedback changes the direction.

I value that process — and the possibility of making things that are both thoughtful and useful.
